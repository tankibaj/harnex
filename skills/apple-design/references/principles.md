# Design principles

last-checked: 2026-10-25
scope: Platform-neutral design principles (Purpose, Agency, Responsibility, Familiarity, Flexibility, Simplicity, Craft, Delight); apply to native and web.

## Design principles ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles))
- Use the 8 principles to weigh competing priorities; no single right way to apply them.

### Purpose: make something meaningful
- Start from intent: identify what matters most to the audience, make those things great.
- Create value: at every stage ask what product is for, whether design serves that.
- Keep focused: prioritize most important features per how people want to use it; make those great. Clear use beats breadth.
- Find new ways to solve the problem: study existing solutions, don't re-create them; define and reflect what sets product apart.

### Agency: let people do things their own way
- Interface exists to help people reach goals: give freedom to act, keep them informed, make mistake recovery easy.
- Stay out of the way: get people straight to the task or content; be unobtrusive, present when needed.
- Give freedom to explore: no lock-in to specific flows or modes; access features freely.
- Guided flow only when necessary; make it easy to skip or escape.
- Help recovery from mistakes: reversible actions, return to previous state; build forgiveness in, make it easy; recovery must not cost time or work.

### Responsibility: act in people's best interest
- Earn trust: prioritize safety and privacy, be transparent about what product does and why.
- Be fully transparent about what product does and why; make intentions clear from first interaction.
- Give clear rationale when asking for permission; be clear what data is collected and how used.
- Keep information safe: collect only what product needs to function; handle with care.
- Anticipate misuse and harm; add protections against abuse and unintended consequences.

### Familiarity: build on what people know
- Ground experience in established physical and digital patterns; apply consistently.
- Use concepts people know from the real world and other software.
- Keep visuals and interactions consistent: once an element has a behavior or appearance, apply it everywhere. Speeds learning, builds confidence.
- Give clear feedback: show when controls are available, indicate when content changes, use system patterns for alerts and choices.

### Flexibility: adapt to diverse contexts and needs
- Support as many devices, interaction types, perspectives as possible.
- Design for everyone: consider diversity of experiences, perspectives, needs; accessibility is a priority from the start; inclusive design for broadest audience.
- Preserve a person's context across platforms and configurations: keep content and controls in consistent, predictable positions; use natural animations to ease transitions.
- Consider a variety of input methods (voice, touch, keyboard, more) so people use what works for them.
- Approach every platform with intention: polished and at home on each, same level of care for each supported platform.

### Simplicity: be clear and direct
- Every element earns its place; remove the unnecessary; logical organization, straightforward navigation.
- Include just what's necessary. Simplicity is not minimalism: focused, useful experience, important things close, others fall away.
- Be concise: simplest wording is often most universal; choose exactly the words needed for concepts and control labels.
- Establish hierarchy: form and function readily apparent; recognizable controls, consistent structure showing where you are and what comes next.

### Craft: care about every detail
- Design reflects care; take time to do the work well.
- Quality sets the tone: be deliberate on each decision; strive for visuals, smooth animation, precise wording, thoughtful audio.
- Experiment and iterate: prototype early, try new approaches, discard what fails, set high bar per feature, refine, retry.
- Test in real-world settings: durable, reliable, high-performing.
- Maintain craft: shipping isn't the finish line; keep interface current with latest platform capabilities and patterns; keep quality bar high.

### Delight: make it human
- People remember how a product makes them feel; choose the emotions right for the experience (energize, calm, thrill) and let them shape design.
- Create defining moments: every interaction (button press to error message) can add character reflecting design spirit.
- Don't mistake delight for decoration: never let delight get in the way of core purpose or the task; balance practical touch vs whimsy by experimenting.
- Consider the whole: delight is the sum of freedom to act, safety to explore, familiar metaphors, flexibility across contexts.
